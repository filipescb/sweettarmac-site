Recording a drive features 1–12

Mount the phone firmly, press one button and drive. Everything below happens while you do.

1

One-tap recording that keeps going

A single Start recording button starts a foreground service that reads the phone's accelerometer, gyroscope and GPS. It keeps running with the screen off or another app in front; the notification has Pause and Stop.

2

Works in any phone orientation

The app removes gravity and measures only the up-and-down movement of the car, wherever and however the phone is mounted. A firm mount, the same every time, gives the best readings.

3

A smoothness score for every second of road

For each GPS second the app takes the median of 0.2-second pieces of the vertical movement, plus the left-right rocking of the car (an uneven pavement at low speed rocks the car without jolting it), so one pothole does not paint a whole road red, and scales it to what it would be at 50 km/h, so the same road scores the same at 30 or 90 km/h. Extra measures describe the texture: wavy roads, fast vibration (cobbles) and lumpy roads.

4

Only measured when it can be measured

Smoothness is recorded from 5 km/h (nothing below that; at the lowest speeds the readings are a little less exact). Fixes with a GPS accuracy worse than 20 m are rejected, and so are seconds with too few sensor readings.

5

Live smoothness panel

While you drive, a panel at the top shows the level of the road right now (for example Good 3.4), your speed and a 60-second chart, refreshed four times a second, in the colour of the level.

6

Live trace and a map that follows you

The drive is drawn as you go, in colour, on its own layer. Your older drives fade back so the current one stands out (more strongly on the light map). The map can turn heading-up, and zooms in closer in town and further out at speed.

7

Hold to stop, pause and resume

Stopping needs a one-second hold on a filling ring, so a stray tap never ends a drive. Pause freezes the clock, and paused time is left out of duration and speed.

8

Looks after forgotten trips

After 30 minutes without moving the trip ends where the car last moved. If you drive off again while paused, recording resumes by itself after a 10-second countdown unless you tap Stay paused.

9

Pauses when the phone is moved

If the gyroscope shows the phone being picked up, swung or knocked out of its mount (more than about 50 degrees of net turn within 1.5 seconds, fast), recording pauses and a pop-up explains that the phone must be in a fixed support to measure accurately.

10

Ignores the phone's own buzzing

The recording notification never vibrates. A detector recognises the phone's vibration motor (one steady frequency between about 70 and 250 Hz) shaking the sensors and leaves that second out, with any bump it seemed to hit.

11

Trip summary after every drive

A card shows distance, time, average speed and the share of each smoothness level, with Show on map and Discard. It closes by itself after ten seconds unless touched, and the last trip can always be discarded.

12

Optional spoken warnings

Say “Rough road ahead” before a stretch you recorded as rough, and “Speed bump ahead” or “Pothole ahead, on the right” before the obstacles the app knows.

The map of your roads features 13–18

Everything you drive becomes a map of how the road feels.

13

Five levels on a 0 to 5 scale

Smooth (4-5), Good (3-4), Fair (2-3), Rough (1-2) and Very rough (0-1), the same on the map, the live panel, the charts and the route cards. A colour-blind palette is available.

14

Each road, in each direction

Roads are measured in cells of about 17 m, separately for each direction of travel (headings within 45 degrees). On a stretch the latest drive counts fully, each earlier one fades with age (a month about 37%, three months 5%), and only the last 10 drives count, so a freshly resurfaced road turns green quickly.

15

Light, dark and auto map

The layout is light or dark, the map is light, dark or switches at sunset (computed on the phone from where you are). Map tiles come from OpenStreetMap, MapTiler or Stadia, as you choose.

16

Tap a road to see what is known

A pop-up shows the level in each direction, how many passes and measurements, when it was last driven, which cars drove it, the surface type (wavy, cobbles, lumpy) and what the other direction is like.

17

Your numbers at a glance

When idle, three figures in a gold frame show the kilometres of road you have recorded (each direction counted separately), the obstacles per km and the change against the previous 30 days. Tap them for details and a six-month chart.

18

Trips list

Every trip can be shown on the map (all others turn grey), moved to another car, or deleted with Undo.

Bumps, holes and manhole covers features 19–26

The things that spoil a drive, found, counted and kept off your route.

19

Finds speed bumps, holes and manhole covers

Short knocks are stored as events and judged against a bar that depends on speed and on your car's stiffness. The app pairs the knock of the front and rear axle, works out length and height, and uses the gyroscope to tell which side was hit and whether the car rolled or pitched.

20

Unavoidable or avoidable

Across repeated drives, an obstacle that is always hit is a speed bump or a long table (counted as 2); one that is sometimes hit, or that drivers steer round, is a hole or cover you can avoid. Once seen only once it stays “unconfirmed” until you drive there again.

21

Notices when you steer round something

On a straight road, a quick move 0.3 to 2.2 m sideways and back, rebuilt from the gyroscope, is read as avoiding a hole or cover.

22

A quick question for big hits

After a very deep hit a card with one Confirm button appears for three seconds. No tap means “no”; it is decided on the phone, so it works with the screen off.

23

Ignores car parks and ramps

Knocks in the first and last 90 seconds at low speed, or in sharp turns, are filtered out as garage ramps and kerbs.

24

Mark them yourself, in gold

Two round gold buttons on the Record tab mark a speed bump or a hole / manhole cover where the car is now (the last GPS fix moved on by speed and heading). Each has its own sound, and your marks are drawn in gold above anything else.

25

Show or hide them, and choose the sources

Two switches (both off at first) show or hide detected bumps and holes on the map; off removes them from the colour key, from the Avoid menu and from the hole question too. Your own gold marks always show. You can also switch off speed bumps from other sources (OpenStreetMap, the server) and holes from the server, to use only yours.

26

Speed bumps and traffic lights from OpenStreetMap

Known bumps are downloaded in map tiles and cached for a month; traffic lights show from zoom level 15. Your own labels always win over them.

Cars and calibration features 27–30

A stiff classic and a soft saloon should call the same road smooth.

27

Up to twelve cars

Each car has a name (up to 12 characters), a colour from twelve clearly different ones, and its own suspension setting. Every reading remembers which car made it; you can switch car with one tap, even while recording.

28

A suspension scale you can feel

Stiffness runs from 1.0× to 6.5×, with Very soft, Soft, Normal (2.5×), Sporty, Stiff, Kart and Rock hard, and a pair of − / + buttons on the Record tab (with a short falling or rising sound) to fine-tune on the road. Changing it re-colours all your old drives instantly.

29

Guided calibration

Calibrate my car asks what the car is like, then fits the setting from roads you know are smooth or rough (pick on the map, use the last 30 seconds, or long-press a road). With a smooth and a rough reference the spread between levels is fitted too.

30

Warns when one of your cars is off

With more than one car, if one constantly reads differently from the others on the same roads, the app tells you which one is probably not calibrated, and offers to calibrate it now. Its readings are adding bad data until then.

Smooth routes, A to B features 31–36

The same roads, chosen for how they feel.

31

Alternatives compared on your data

The router (OSRM, openrouteservice or GraphHopper) gives alternatives and the app adds forced detours; every candidate is checked every 15 m against your roads, by direction, with penalties for bumps and holes. Cards show time, distance, share of smooth road, bumps and holes.

32

Fastest or smoothest

A Smooth slider sets how much feel matters against time. Roads you have not driven count as average.

33

Search, pins and the map menu

Search with suggestions for From and To; long-press the map for Route here, Start from here, a reference road for your car, or to save the place. The pop-up opens in the middle of the visible map so it never lands behind a button.

34

Home and favourites

A Home button and up to 12 favourites sit on the Route tab for one-tap planning; long-press a favourite to rename it.

35

The chosen route stands out

When results are shown, the recorded roads fade back and the chosen route is drawn thicker with a white halo.

36

See progress, and cancel

While the phone or the server works, a box shows what is happening, the seconds gone and a progress bar, with a Cancel button that drops the request.

The Loop features 37–46

“Take me for a drive, and bring me back.” The reason the app exists.

37

A round trip by time or distance

Choose a time (10 minutes to 2 hours) or a distance (up to 200 km / 125 miles) and a tolerance of ±5 to 25%. The Loop starts from where you are, with no destination to enter.

38

Starts and ends exactly where you are

Whatever the router returns, both ends of every loop are put back on your position (a route that starts or ends more than 1 km away is thrown out). The map draws the loop from the Start / finish flag.

39

Over roads that are known and smooth

The recorded cells form a graph; the planner searches it for a loop of the right size, hands the router a few via points, and requires at least 95% known road whenever that is possible.

40

Always a loop with the least unknown road

Even with little recorded road, the planner also tries there-and-back loops, out along recorded roads and back along the same ones, so that the loop with the least unknown road is always offered.

41

A strict order of priorities

1) 95 to 100% known road, the more the better; 2) the smoothness you asked for; 3) the time or distance within your tolerance; 4) as few of the things you avoid as possible. Loops outside the tolerance only fill up to three results.

42

What to avoid

Choose any of traffic lights, speed bumps, big holes and manhole covers, zebra crossings and tolls. Each light, crossing, toll, bump or hole is worth a 250 m detour in the search, and each loop lists what it still has.

43

Explorer: roads you have not driven

A green switch draws a loop over roads you have not recorded yet, for the time or distance chosen, and turns the interface green so you know the mode. Drive it with recording on and the map grows.

44

Explorer on routes

The Route tab has an Explorer button (at the right end of the favourites line) and, in Options, a share from 10% to 100%: the part of the ride that must be on roads you have never recorded, with any car. The button turns green and says "Find smoothest route and explore 40% of unknown". The routes shown are the fastest ones with at least that much new road; if the road network cannot give that much, it says so and shows the closest.

45

Loop cards that tell you what you get

Each card shows time, distance, the share of smooth and good road, the share unknown (when there is some), lights, zebra crossings, bumps, holes and the very rough kilometres. The top loop is tagged Recommended; the list folds away to leave the map.

46

Never fewer than three

When at least three loops exist, you get at least three, with the unknown share shown on any that is over the 5% limit.

Handing the route to a navigator features 47–49

The app plans; your favourite navigator guides.

47

Google Maps that follows the planned route

Google takes at most nine stops and picks the roads between them itself, so the app adds stops where a check router drifts from the planned route until they agree (at least 97% overlap), and tells you how well it matches.

48

Choose your navigator: OsmAnd, Organic Maps, Google Maps or any app

One Navigate button remembers your choice: OsmAnd (recommended) or Organic Maps open the exact route as a GPX track; Google Maps gets up to 9 stops that keep it on your roads; other apps take a destination only.

49

Offers to record the drive

When you send a route to a navigator, a card asks Start recording this drive? with Yes and No; with no answer in two seconds it is Yes.

Your data features 50–53

It is yours, on your phone, and it can all be taken with you.

50

Everything is stored on the phone

Readings, trips, obstacles, cars, settings and places live in a database on the phone. Offline mode, the default, sends nothing anywhere except requests for maps, routes and place search to the services you choose.

51

A full backup in one file

Back up everything writes one file with every recording and trip, the obstacles and your corrections, the cars with their calibration, all settings, your user id, your server id and your nickname. Restoring it on a new phone or after reinstalling puts everything back as it was, including your place in the explorers ranking. Keep the file private. (Export GeoJSON is still there for maps and other tools.)

52

Delete what you want

Delete the last hours, days or trips, or everything. Your own routing and map keys and the anonymous online id are never put in an export.

53

No account, just a nickname

The app makes its own random user id and asks for a nickname when you first open it. No e-mail address and no Google account. The id labels your own data; the nickname is only sent if you switch on online mode.

Online mode: drivers helping drivers features 54–59

Optional, off at first, anonymous, and deletable.

54

Anonymous, trimmed uploads

After each trip the readings are sent with the first and last 300 m cut, positions rounded to about 1 m, only the day (not the time), the score already adjusted for your car's calibration, and a random id made on the phone (not your e-mail). Trips under about 800 m send nothing.

55

Anonymous facts about the trip

With each trip: the date, Android version, country, language, suspension setting, the number of vehicles you use (never their names), your options from a fixed list (no addresses, places, e-mail or keys) and counts of how often you used each feature. The dialog that switches it on says so.

56

The server plans on everybody's data

In online mode, routes and loops are planned by the server with the same algorithm and everyone's data; if it cannot be reached the phone plans on its own and says so.

57

Bad data is kept out

On the server, readings far from the middle of the normal curve for their stretch of road are put in quarantine and not used for the averages everybody gets. Nothing is deleted.

58

Explorers ranking

Choose a unique nickname and open Explorers ranking in the menu: the drivers who registered the most new road (road nobody had sent before) come first, with medals for the top three, their nickname and the kilometres. Highways and freeways do not count (they are almost all smooth; the aim is to record streets and roads). The golden steering wheel, animated and sparkly, goes to the one with the most new smooth and good road in the last 30 days, as long as the car is calibrated; if it is not, the app says so and offers the calibration routine.

59

In your hands

Send now and Delete my data on the server sit side by side in Options. Deleting removes everything your device sent, including the trip facts.

Made to be used in a car features 60–63

Big targets, clear colours, your language.

60

Six languages, km or miles

English, Português, Español, Français, Deutsch and Italiano, chosen from the phone's language; distances and speeds in km or miles (and mph).

61

Accessible by design

48 px touch targets, text that follows the phone's font size, a colour-blind palette, light and dark layouts, portrait only, and edge-to-edge screens with the new back gesture.

62

Sounds you can choose

Marking a bump and a hole each has its own sound, and the suspension − / + a very short falling and rising one. Options has four choices for each (or off), played when you pick.

63

Options that stay out of the way

Options always opens folded, with the map blurred behind it, and ten sections by what you want to do (My cars, While driving, Map & roads, Routes, Community, App & language, My data, Advanced, About), a search box and a status line on My cars and Community.

Safe by design features 64–65

64

Set it up, then drive

Mount the phone, set the app up before you set off, and never handle it while driving. Big buttons and short holds are there so that you do not have to look.

65

No ads, no analytics, no tracking

The app has no advertising and no analytics. The only thing that ever leaves the phone is what the Privacy section of this guide lists.
